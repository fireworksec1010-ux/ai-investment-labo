Interest rates

An interest rate is the "rental fee" for money. People who borrow money pay back extra based on the rate. You earn interest on bank deposits because you are, in effect, lending money to the bank.

Policy rates

1. What does this number mean?

It is the rate a country's central bank (the Bank of Japan in Japan, the Federal Reserve in the U.S.) targets to manage the economy and prices. It is the rate banks pay when they lend to each other for just one day, and it spreads to mortgage and deposit rates.

Economics in 30 seconds

Why do central banks raise and lower rates?

When prices rise too fast, they raise rates so borrowing is harder and spending slows down a little. When the economy is weak, they lower rates so borrowing is easier and money keeps moving. It works like a car's brake and gas pedal.

10-year bond yields (long-term rates)

1. What does this number mean?

A government bond is an IOU a country issues when it borrows money. The 10-year bond yield shows how much interest per year you get if you buy a bond that is repaid in 10 years. It is also called the "long-term interest rate" and changes every day with buying and selling in the market.

2. What happens when rates go up?

When rates go upWhen rates go down
Deposits tend to earn more interestDeposits tend to earn less interest
Mortgage and loan payments tend to riseMortgage and loan payments tend to fall
Borrowing gets harder for companies — often a headwind for stock pricesBorrowing gets easier for companies — often a tailwind for stock prices
That country's currency tends to become popular (U.S. rates up → stronger dollar, weaker yen)That country's currency tends to become less popular

In reality many factors, like the economy and company results, overlap, so it does not always work this way.

Economics in 30 seconds

Why is the "Japan–U.S. rate gap" always in the news?

Say a deposit earns 1% in Japan and 4% in the U.S. Many people would rather keep money in the U.S. To do that they must change yen into dollars, so dollars get bought and the yen tends to weaken. Compare how the gap in these charts widens and narrows with how USD/JPY moves.

4. Sources and data

Unit: % per year. Values from the last business day of each month, not daily values. Japan's policy rate is the actual uncollateralized overnight call rate that the Bank of Japan targets. The U.S. policy rate is the actual (effective) federal funds rate, for which the Fed sets a target range.
