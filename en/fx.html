Exchange rates

An exchange rate is the price for swapping yen into another currency. "$1 = ¥150" means you need 150 yen to get one dollar.

USD/JPY (U.S. dollar and Japanese yen)

1. What does this number mean?

It shows how many yen you need to buy one dollar. When the number gets bigger, the yen is "weaker" (worth less). When it gets smaller, the yen is "stronger" (worth more).

Economics in 30 seconds

¥150 became ¥160 — so why is the yen "weaker"?

Imagine a $1 snack. Before, you could buy it for ¥150. Now you need ¥160. You need more yen to buy the same thing, so the yen has lost power — it is "weaker." The number goes up, but we call it a weak yen because we are looking at the yen's value.

2. What happens when it changes?

When the yen gets weakerWhen the yen gets stronger
Imports (wheat, gasoline, etc.) tend to cost moreImports tend to cost less
Travel abroad tends to cost moreTravel abroad tends to cost less
Companies that sell overseas tend to earn moreCompanies that sell overseas tend to earn less
Foreign stocks and funds tend to be worth more in yenForeign stocks and funds tend to be worth less in yen

We say "tend to" because other things (like material costs or company efforts) also change the result.

Economics in 30 seconds

Why do exchange rates move?

There are many reasons, but a big one is the gap in interest rates between countries. Money in a country with higher interest earns more, so that currency becomes popular and its price tends to rise. Trade, the economy and reactions to news also move rates every day.

EUR/JPY (euro and Japanese yen)

The euro is the shared currency used by many European countries, such as Germany, France and Italy. This shows "how many yen for one euro," and you read it the same way as USD/JPY.

4. Sources and data
