Economics in 30 seconds

Neither one is "right"

Saving gives you safety. Investing gives you a chance to grow — in exchange for accepting that it can also shrink. Many people keep living money in savings and invest part of the money they won't need for years. The right balance depends on your age, income and plans.

Inflation and the value of money

Economics in 30 seconds

When prices go up, money is worth less

If bread cost ¥100 last year and ¥110 this year, your ¥100 can no longer buy it. The amount is the same, but it buys less — so the value of money went down. That is inflation.

Risk and return

Economics in 30 seconds

In investing, "risk" means how much results swing

Risk in investing is not "danger" — it is how widely the results can swing. Return is what you gain (or lose). In general, things that can grow a lot can also fall a lot. Something with "low risk and high return" usually does not exist.

Spread it out (diversify)

Investing in many companies and countries, not just one, makes the hit smaller when one of them falls.

Hold for long (long term)

Prices swing a lot in the short run, but over long periods the swings tend to even out. Still, growth is never guaranteed.

Buy a little at a time

Investing the same amount every month buys less when prices are high and more when they are low, which reduces the effect of timing.

Economics in 30 seconds

What does "investing is your own responsibility" mean?

When you invest, your money can grow or shrink. You decide what to buy, when, and how much — and you live with the result.

That is why it matters not to decide only on someone's "recommendation," but to check official information yourself. Japan's Financial Services Agency and J-FLEC (Japan Financial Literacy and Education Corporation) also offer basic money education.
