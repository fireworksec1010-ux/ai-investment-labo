Economics in 30 seconds

What is GDP (gross domestic product)?

It is the total value of all the new goods and services made in a country in one year. It is used to compare the size of economies. To compare countries, everything here is converted to U.S. dollars — so when the yen gets weaker, Japan's GDP looks smaller in dollars.

Inflation = how much prices rose in a year

2% inflation means something that cost 100 yen last year costs about 102 yen now, on average. Many central banks aim for "about 2% a year." Too high makes life hard; below zero (deflation, when prices keep falling) makes it hard for profits and wages to grow.

Economics in 30 seconds

Rate gaps move exchange rates

Money in a country with higher interest rates earns more, so its currency tends to be popular. When the gap between U.S. and Japanese rates is wide, people tend to sell yen and buy dollars, which can weaken the yen. But exchange rates also move with the economy, trade and news, so it does not always work this way.
