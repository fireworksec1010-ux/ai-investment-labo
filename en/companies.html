Sales, profit and market value

Economics in 30 seconds

Think of a ramen shop

If the shop sells 10,000 bowls of ¥1,000 ramen in a year, its sales are ¥10 million. Take away ingredients, rent and wages, and what is left is operating profit. Take away taxes and other costs, and the final amount is net profit. And the price the market would put on "buying the whole shop" is its market value.

WordMeaningWhere to find it
Sales (revenue)All the money that came in from selling goods and services. Shows the company's size.Financial report
Operating profitSales minus the costs of the main business (materials, wages, advertising). Shows how well the main business earns.Financial report
Net profitWhat is left after everything, including interest and taxes. Dividends come from this.Financial report
Market valueShare price × number of shares. What the market thinks the whole company is worth, including hopes for the future. Changes every day.Calculated from the share price

A company with big sales does not always have big profit or market value. A supermarket can have huge sales but earns only a little on each item. Software companies often earn a lot compared with their sales.

Market value changes every day with the share price. This site does not handle share price data, so please check market values on each company's investor relations (IR) page or a stock exchange.

Apple vs. Microsoft

Apple

Sells devices like iPhone, Mac and iPad, plus services like the App Store, music and cloud storage. Most of its sales come from products it designs itself.

Microsoft

Sells software like Windows and Office, and cloud services for businesses (Azure). Services for companies and governments are a big part of its business.

Their fiscal years end in different months (Apple in September, Microsoft in June), so the periods do not line up exactly.

Sources and data

Numbers come from the XBRL data (machine-readable financial data) in the annual reports each company filed with the SEC (Form 10-K for U.S. companies, Form 20-F for Japanese companies). Sales use tags such as Revenues, operating profit uses OperatingIncomeLoss and similar tags, and net profit is the amount attributable to the parent company's shareholders. Companies use different accounting standards (U.S. GAAP or IFRS), so treat comparisons as a guide. Check each company's IR page for the latest results.
